# Examples: before and after in the economics register

Synthetic sentences in the style of a theory paper. Each "after" keeps every claim, symbol and
citation of its "before". The source of each fix is in brackets.

**1. Formulaic opener, novelty padding, inflation.**
Before: *In recent years, the question of how rules shape information acquisition has attracted
increasing attention. To the best of our knowledge, this paper is the first to delve into this
crucial and intricate interplay, paving the way for a novel understanding of incomplete contracts.*
After: *Rules are written in words, and words have a letter and a spirit. We study an employee who
decides what to find out about a case before acting under a rule that either permits the action or
says nothing about it.*
[F5 opener; F4 novelty; C1 delve, crucial, intricate, interplay; C2 paving the way; style-corpus
§1.1, §1.4]

**2. Not X but Y, one-line closer.**
Before: *The permission does not change the document. It changes the cost of acting without
knowing. This distinction is the heart of the paper.*
After: *The permission does nothing to the document; what it changes is the cost to the employee of
sending it without knowing.*
[A1 kept as a genuine correction of a reader's belief, merged into one sentence; A2 closer cut]

**3. Shallow rider, copula avoidance, borrowed authority.**
Before: *The contact posteriors serve as the key object of the analysis, highlighting the role of
information costs, as experts have long argued.*
After: *The contact posteriors are the object the wording moves. They do not depend on the prior,
which determines only how often each is reached.*
[C5 serves as; C3 highlighting; C6 experts; §4.3 interpretive sentence]

**4. Connective stacking, boilerplate emphasis, hedging by vagueness.**
Before: *Moreover, the result is somewhat robust. Furthermore, it is worth noting that it holds
for a relatively wide class of costs. Additionally, importantly, it does not require wiggle room.*
After: *The reversal holds for every uniformly posterior-separable cost of information and does not
require doubt to be an excuse.*
[F6, F10, F9; the claim's scope named instead of "somewhat", "relatively"]

**5. Clause stack, nominalization.**
Before: *The determination of the threshold by the agent, which depends on the wording of the rule
that the principal has chosen, while also being affected by the cost of information, which we take
to be posterior separable, is the subject of this section.*
After: *This section solves the agent's problem for a given wording. The agent chooses a threshold.
It depends on the wording and on the cost of information, which we take to be posterior
separable.*
[F11 split; F12 "determination" to "chooses"; §2.1 claim then support]

**6. Missing intuition after a theorem.**
Before: *Theorem 2 states that, when the shelter is ordered and the agent investigates under either
wording, a permission raises both contacts. We now turn to the organization's problem.*
After: *Theorem 2 states that, when the shelter is ordered and the agent investigates under either
wording, a permission raises both contacts. The intuition is simple. The permission lowers the cost
of acting on a given belief, so the agent is willing to act on weaker evidence of safety; and it
lowers that cost faster the more suspicious he is, so he also needs stronger evidence of harm before
he gives up. We now turn to the organization's problem.*
[Voice: §2.3; content taken from the theorem's own statement and the paper's earlier text, nothing
new]

**7. Strong claim without its boundary.**
Before: *The organization therefore prefers silence in lower-risk categories and permission in
higher-risk ones.*
After: *The organization therefore prefers silence in lower-risk categories and permission in
higher-risk ones. This does not say that every organization should permit more in risky categories:
the ordering holds within the range in which the agent investigates under either wording, and for
an intermediate weight on beneficial uses.*
[Voice: §3.5, with the conditions copied from the theorem's hypotheses]

**8. Elegant variation, repeated opener.**
Before: *The agent acquires a signal. The employee then updates. The worker acts if the posterior is
below the lower contact. The decision maker refrains otherwise.*
After: *The agent acquires a signal with two outcomes and updates on it. If the posterior is at the
lower contact he acts; at the upper contact he refrains.*
[F14 one name per object; B2 openers merged]

**9. Forced triad and sales language in a literature paragraph.**
Before: *A rich, vibrant and growing literature has explored incomplete contracts, bounded
rationality and complexity. Our work builds on, extends and complements these seminal
contributions.*
After: *The principal's side connects to the literature that asks why contracts are incomplete.
Anderlini and Felli (1994, 2000) show that the inability to describe states does not by itself
generate incompleteness, because an approximately optimal contract can always be written;
incompleteness requires that writing be costly.*
[B1 triads; C1 rich, vibrant; F8 cite the paper that matters and say what it did; §3.9]

**10. Over-claiming verb in an empirical sentence.**
Before: *The experiment proves that permissions cause harm.*
After: *In the treatment sessions, subjects given a permission shared sensitive documents more often
than subjects given no clause (Table 2).*
[F1; the number pointer is kept only if the table exists in the source. Never invent one.]
