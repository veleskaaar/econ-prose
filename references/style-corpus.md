# Style corpus: how the model papers write

Distilled from the papers listed in `corpus-index.md`. Each pattern carries a source tag; a pattern
is in this file only if it recurs (in two papers, or in two sections of one paper). Exemplars are
short quotations for study, not text to paste. Use the patterns the way the papers do: one at a
time, where the content calls for it.

Source tags: AF00 Anderlini and Felli; GGS25 Ghosh, Goswami and Sarkar; CDM11 Caplin, Dean and
Martin; CD15 Caplin and Dean; CM15 Caplin and Martin; CLR20 Chambers, Liu and Rehbeck; DEGO26 Dean,
Enke, Graeber and Ortoleva; EG23 Enke and Graeber; EGOY24 Enke, Graeber, Oprea and Yang; DM16 Dean
and Martin; ENKE-R Enke's review of the cognitive turn.

## 1. Opening moves

**1.1 The first sentence is a flat, general, uncited declarative.** No hedge, no citation, no
"in recent years".
- "Apparently mistaken decisions are ubiquitous." (CD15)
- "Limits on attention impact choice." (CD15)
- "Many everyday decisions are made without full examination of all available options, and as a
  result, the best available option may be missed." (CDM11)
- "Acquiring information is an integral part of decisions under uncertainty." (CLR20)
- "There is a standard approach to modelling signal processing and choice." (CM15)
- "Behavioral economics has made economics more realistic." (EGOY24)
- "Arguably the most pervasive assumption in economics is that agents are rational in the sense
  that..." (DM16)

**1.2 The second move is a tension or a question.**
- "To what extent does this reflect irrationality, as opposed to a rational trade-off between the
  costs of information acquisition and the expected benefits of learning?" (CD15)
- "However, little is known about how such incomplete consideration affects choice behavior." (CDM11)
- "An important open question is how to test a model of optimal behavior in the face of costly
  information." (CD15)
- "From an applied point of view, the devil is in the details." (CM15)
- "However, one of the costs of this realism is a reduction in parsimony." (EGOY24)

**1.3 Three parallel cited examples establish a phenomenon.** Same syntax three times, one citation
each, then the general claim.
- "Shoppers may buy unnecessarily expensive products due to their failure to notice whether or not
  sales tax is included in stated prices (Chetty, Looney and Kroft (2009)). Buyers of second-hand
  cars focus their attention on the leftmost digit of the odometer (Lacetera, Pope and Sydnor
  (2012)). Purchasers limit their attention to a relatively small number of websites..." (CD15)

**1.4 The contribution arrives within two paragraphs, first person plural, one strong verb.**
develop, provide, characterize, identify, document, propose, show, study.
- "We develop a search-theoretic choice experiment to study the impact of incomplete consideration
  on the quality of choices." (CDM11)
- "We provide a characterisation of choice behaviour generated by a Bayesian expected utility
  maximiser." (CM15)
- "This paper proposes an alternative view: reference dependence arises because reference points
  provide comparative information to decision makers who are unsure how to value objects or
  actions." (DEGO26)
- "In this paper, we study a general model of costly information acquisition that allows for
  interactions between the information cost and the expected payoff from the decision problem."
  (CLR20)

**1.5 The central result is named once, early, and plainly.**
- "Our central finding is that many decisions can be understood using the satisficing model of
  Simon [1955]." (CDM11)
- "Our main result is that these conditions are both necessary and sufficient for any arbitrary
  finite data set to be consistent with a model of costly information acquisition." (CD15)
- "The main idea consists of two components. (i) ... (ii) ..." (EG23)
- "The key to our approach is the observation that one can learn much about a DM's attention
  strategy from state dependent stochastic choice data." (CD15)

**1.6 The idea in one sentence, then a concrete first-person micro-example.**
- "The idea behind this paper is that such comparisons provide information that, in the presence
  of value uncertainty, generates reference dependence." (DEGO26)
- "For instance, I may be unable to assess the absolute utility of making $90 today, but I know
  that it is less than the utility of the $100 I typically make." (DEGO26)
- "Suppose I must choose between two new jobs relative to my current one (the reference)." (DEGO26)
- "Intuitively, if you don't know how to construct an optimum, you also don't know how the optimum
  changes with fundamentals." (EGOY24)

## 2. Paragraph architecture

**2.1 Short topic sentence, long support.** A claim of 5 to 12 words, then one or two sentences of
25 to 40 words that give the reason or the mechanism.
- "The logic is familiar. The wording matters only for cases near the agent's threshold, and in a
  low-risk category those are mostly cases in which acting is worth its risk to the organization."
  (thesis draft 20, already in the corpus voice)

**2.2 Run-in bold headings organize the introduction's summary.** Each names a topic in one to four
words or asks a question; the paragraph then answers it.
- "Setup." "Why comparative information?" "Posteriors after comparative information." "Contrast,
  attenuation and coherent arbitrariness." "Theory of endogenous reference points." "Caution."
  "Reference dependence as information and / or preferences?" "Related literature." (DEGO26)
- "Study design." "Evidence for behavioral attenuation." "Simple points and diminishing
  sensitivity." "Behavioral anomalies." "Limits of attenuation." "Contribution and related
  literature." (EGOY24)
- "Objectives and characteristics." "Caveats." "Overview of broad lines of inquiry." (ENKE-R)

**2.3 Formal statement, then a fixed-opener intuition sentence.**
- "The intuition is simple. Reference points that are too low or too high will almost always
  yield the same ordinal comparison, which would be uninformative." (DEGO26)
- "The intuition behind Proposition 1 is not hard to outline. First of all notice that..." (AF00)
- "Intuitively, uncertainty induces belief compression toward the prior, leading to attenuation,
  except around the reference point, where ordinal information produces a contrast effect." (DEGO26)
- "To illustrate, consider the case of a '+' ordinal signal." (DEGO26)

**2.4 Decomposition openers.** "First, ... Second, ... Third, ..." as paragraph or sentence starts;
"(i) ... (ii) ... (iii)" inside a sentence; "The posterior mean ... is the sum of two components.
First, an absolute term... Second, a relative component..." (DEGO26)
- "Deriving reference dependence from information theory offers three main advantages. First, ...
  Second, ... Third, ..." (DEGO26)
- "This cognitive uncertainty elicitation has five main features. (i) ... (ii) ... (iii) ... (iv)
  ... (v) ..." (EG23)

**2.5 A running example introduced once and reused.** Red and blue balls (CD15), jurors (CM15),
an oil company deciding where to drill (CLR20), wine and pasta packets (DEGO26), document sharing
(thesis). The example is introduced with "Consider", "Suppose", or "By way of example, consider".

## 3. Sentence patterns

**3.1 Question, then the answer in the next sentence.**
- "Are there any patterns of choice error that cannot be explained by some underlying information
  cost function, or is such a general theory vacuous? We answer this question by characterizing all
  patterns of stochastic choice consistent with rational decision making in the face of information
  costs." (CD15)
- "Which reference point provides more information? The status quo is the most informative
  reference point if and only if its bias satisfies..." (DEGO26)
- "Does this combination lead to over- or under-estimation? The answer is both, depending on how
  close u_x is to u_r." (DEGO26)
- "What happens with an ordinal signal? The belief gets truncated." (DEGO26)
- "Is it the case that ... ? Or is it the case that ... ? The rest of this section is devoted to
  showing that the answer to the latter question is yes." (AF00)

**3.2 Naming a concept.**
- "We call this second property shelter." (thesis) / "As in Anderlini and Felli (1994), we call
  this the Approximation Result." (AF00)
- "We say a revealed preference cycle is 'broken' if a revealed preference relation is removed from
  that cycle." (DM16)
- "To emphasize the potential interaction between expected utility and cost, we refer to such model
  as a nonseparable costly information acquisition model." (CLR20)
- "(see Caplin and Martin (2015), henceforth CM15)" (CD15)

**3.3 Declared agnosticism and deliberate exclusion.**
- "We are agnostic about whether this uncertainty reflects preference uncertainty, aggregation
  uncertainty, or something else." (DEGO26)
- "We remain agnostic about the source of the uncertainty." (DEGO26)
- "We deliberately exclude such preferences to isolate the explanatory power of information." (DEGO26)
- "We are agnostic over whether the strong correlations ... reflect a causal effect of the true
  noise ... or whether awareness of potential errors itself drives behaviors." (EG23)

**3.4 Scope of a claim stated plainly, often with "nests" or "encompasses".**
- "Since we make no specific assumptions about the costs and constraints that the decision maker
  faces when gathering information, our tests encompass all existing models of optimal information
  acquisition." (CD15)
- "We put no restrictions on the cost function, meaning that our model nests all standard models of
  information acquisition." (CD15)
- "Apart from the standard assumptions of expected utility maximization and Bayesian updating, the
  only additional assumption of the model is that the decision maker prefers higher expected
  payoffs." (CLR20)

**3.5 What a result does and does not say.**
- "Remark 3 does not assert that all possible computable contracts will be incomplete for the class
  of contracting problems that it identifies. This is in fact not the case for the following
  reason." (AF00)
- "Notice that Remark 2 is silent as to whether the incompleteness that it identifies really
  matters in terms of the agents' expected utility levels." (AF00)
- "Importantly, our hypothesis is not only that these decisions are relatively insensitive, which
  can in principle occur for many reasons, including certain preferences. Rather, the hypothesis of
  behavioral attenuation is that..." (EGOY24)
- "Of course, this does not rule out the existence of genuine reference-dependent preferences or
  loss aversion." (DEGO26)
- "This theory of endogenous reference points is an as-if account: it does not require individuals
  to consciously choose reference points." (DEGO26)
- "While Theorem 1 does not require this assumption, in applications it provides much of the bite of
  the NIAS inequalities." (CM15)

**3.6 Contrast frames.**
- "While the necessity of the NIAS inequalities is clear, the sufficiency of the inequalities is
  more surprising." (CM15)
- "Unlike existing measures of rationality, it responds to both the number and severity of revealed
  preference violations." (DM16)
- "On the one hand it is true that in some cases the first best sharing rule cannot be embodied in
  an algorithmic contract. On the other hand, given any contracting problem and any arbitrarily
  small number ε, there exists..." (AF00)
- "the observed generosity of dictators is driven not so much by their preferences for fairness but
  by proclivity for their image concerns" (GGS25)
- "Our results generalize the No Improving Attention Cycles condition of Caplin et al. (2015) in the
  same way that the Generalized Axiom of Revealed Preference generalizes the cyclic monotonicity
  condition of Rockafellar (1966)." (CLR20)

**3.7 Meta-signposts.** Short, conventional, once each.
- "Two remarks are in order." / "Before proceeding, two remarks are in order." (DEGO26)
- "Two caveats are in order." (ENKE-R)
- "Some additional notation is needed at this point." (AF00)
- "We are now ready to define the set of computable contracts." / "We are now ready to ask formally
  the question of whether..." (AF00)
- "We conclude this Section with an observation." (AF00)
- "Without further delay, we define revealed posteriors and revealed information structures." (CLR20)
- "To sum up," (DEGO26) / "Summarizing," (EGOY24) / "Taken together, these two findings characterize
  the satisficing model." (CDM11)

**3.8 Framing a negative or limiting result without apology.**
- "The Approximation Result can be viewed as a negative result in establishing the link between
  bounded rationality and contract incompleteness. Effectively, it seems to tell us that, at least
  in the limit case, bounded rationality in the formulation of the contract alone cannot generate
  any meaningful form of contractual incompleteness." (AF00)
- "We find mixed evidence for both." (EGOY24)
- "Of course this assumption greatly limits the applicability of our approach." (AF00)

**3.9 Literature sentences: one clause of likeness, one of difference.**
- "Our paper is most closely related to that of de Oliveira et al. (2013), which derives similar
  results in the setting of choice over menus." (CD15)
- "The closest antecedent is Villas-Boas (2024), who also models a DM who receives an ordinal signal
  relative to a reference point, but does not examine the applications or endogenous reference
  points we consider." (DEGO26)
- "Our model should be viewed as a direct generalization of this contribution." (CLR20)
- "Unlike this work, our article is based on the study of unobservable information that is
  'relevant to the rational assessment of the alternatives and thus should not be regarded as a
  frame'..." (CM15)
- "Relative to existing models of value uncertainty or cognitive noise (...), we model reference
  points as information." (DEGO26)
- "Our main contribution to this literature is to document that cognitive uncertainty predicts
  biases across various economic decision tasks..." (EG23)

**3.10 Roadmap.** One sentence per section, same verb family, then the proofs sentence.
- "Section 2 introduces the costly information representation. Section 3 provides our key
  characterization theorem. Section 4 establishes limits on the identifiability of information
  costs. Section 5 reviews related literature." (CD15)
- "The paper proceeds as follows. ... Finally, Section 7 contains our concluding remarks. Proofs are
  relegated to the appendix." (CLR20)
- "For ease of exposition, all proofs are relegated to the Appendix." (AF00)

## 4. Model-section habits

**4.1 Primitives in one sentence.**
- "A decision problem is defined by a quadruple (A, X, Ω, l), where A is a finite set of actions, X
  is a finite set of prizes, Ω is a finite set of states, and l is the probability distribution
  (prior) over states." (CM15)
- "We consider a decision maker (DM) who chooses among actions, the outcomes of which depend on
  which of a finite number of states of the world eventuates." (CD15)
- "Two risk-averse agents face a risk-sharing problem." (AF00)

**4.2 A motivating sentence before each definition.**
- "We assume that there is a cost associated with the use of each information structure.
  DEFINITION 3: An information cost function is a mapping..." (CD15)
- "We now provide definitions necessary to discuss the ex-ante payoff. The ex-ante payoff is the
  utility an individual expects to receive for a given information structure." (CLR20)

**4.3 An interpretive sentence after each displayed formula.**
- "Here the first bracketed term is the probability of each signal, and the second is the maximum
  achievable expected utility from A given the resulting beliefs." (CD15)
- "In this definition, B is the set of removed relations, and p_x(q_x - q_y) is the cost of
  removing relation q_x R_0 q_y." (DM16)
- "In other words, π is a garbling of ρ when there is a stochastic matrix B that can be applied to
  ρ that yields π." (CLR20)
- "The posterior mean after the cardinal and ordinal signals is the sum of two components." (DEGO26)

**4.4 Assumption openers and notation housekeeping.**
- "For simplicity we assume that the expected utility function u : X → R is known." (CD15)
- "For tractability, we assume that the prior about each u_{x,i} is distributed according to..."
  (DEGO26)
- "Throughout the paper, we denote by z̃ the mean of the belief about the random variable z." (DEGO26)
- "When no confusion arises, we will drop the subscript x." (DEGO26)
- "Unless we note otherwise, S is assumed to be the set of natural numbers N." (AF00)
- "Notation is consistent with Caplin et al. (2015) whenever possible for ease of comparison." (CLR20)
- "It is important to notice at this point that all the results in Sections 3, 4, 5 and 6 below
  apply (in some cases trivially) to the case of finitely many states." (AF00)

**4.5 Generality remarks placed right after the assumption.**
- "All the results in this paper generalize to a set-up like the one used in Anderlini and Felli
  (1994)." (AF00)
- "We focus on Normal priors and signals for simplicity. ... while the exact functional forms of
  our results rely on Normality, the core intuition generalizes." (DEGO26)
- "While we focus on the case of a static, once-off choice of information structure, we show in
  Caplin and Dean (2014) that our results extend directly to the case of sequential choice of
  information." (CD15)

**4.6 A convenience restatement after a hard definition.**
- "Definition 1 can be cumbersome to use since it involves constructing an entire set of partitions
  Π(x), and then checking whether P* belongs to the set. Fortunately, there is an equivalent way to
  state Definition 1 that is easier to use." (AF00)

**4.7 Prose proof sketch in numbered steps.**
- "The proof of the necessity of the NIAS inequalities follows directly from the definition of a BEU
  representation. ... The sufficiency of the NIAS inequalities is established by constructing a BEU
  representation based on any utility function that satisfies the NIAS inequalities. The first step
  is to construct a perception function using the observed data. ... The second step is to determine
  a choice function that rationalises the data..." (CM15)
- "The second observation that clarifies the logic behind Proposition 1 is the following. ... To
  close the argument it is then enough to notice that..." (AF00)

**4.8 Named results and labeled remarks.**
- "Remark 1. Incompleteness and First Best:" "Proposition 1. The Approximation Result:" (AF00)
- "Lemma 1 is proved in Caplin et al. (2015). The lemma says that if an information structure is
  consistent with the state dependent stochastic choice dataset, then the revealed information
  structure is a garbling." (CLR20)

**4.9 Describing a definition's ambition and its limits.**
- "Of course, from a formal point of view, the definition we propose here applies to the model that
  we analyze and does not directly translate into a universally applicable one. However, we believe
  that it can be usefully extended to fit a wide variety of models." (AF00)

## 5. Results and empirical prose

**5.1 Hypotheses stated with a fixed verb.** "We posit that" (GGS25), "We hypothesize that" (GGS25,
EGOY24), "Our hypothesis is that" (EGOY24), "We conjectured that" (EGOY24).

**5.2 Magnitudes in prose with one anchor number per sentence.**
- "subjects fail to select the best option almost 38 percent of the time, and leave $3.12, or 17
  percent of the maximum amount on the table in each round." (CDM11)
- "In 93% of our baseline experiments we find that higher CU is associated with a lower elasticity
  of decisions to economic fundamentals." (EGOY24)
- "In the running example it survives when one employee in twelve follows the letter and is lost
  when one in six does." (thesis)

**5.3 Interpretation marked as interpretation.**
- "We interpret our results as suggesting that classic behavioral economics both applies more
  broadly than previously acknowledged and that it is more unified than we might have thought."
  (EGOY24)
- "This suggests two interpretations. First, ... Second, ..." (EGOY24)
- "One view in the literature ... is that these phenomena reflect domain-specific biases or even
  preferences. Another view is that they have a common origin." (EG23)
- "The overall picture is clear:" (ENKE-R) / "What is perhaps most striking is that we find the same
  pattern across a wide variety of choice domains." (EGOY24)
- "This suggests that our subjects may be satisficing for the reasons that Simon [1955] originally
  proposed, as a rule of thumb that performs adequately across a broad range of environments, rather
  than finely honing their search strategy to each choice environment they face." (CDM11)

**5.4 Paragraph openers that report.** "We find that" (CDM11, repeatedly), "We also find", "We
observe that" (GGS25), "Our results demonstrate the importance of" (DM16, once).

## 6. Lexicon

**Mechanism verbs:** generate, produce, induce, give rise to, yield, render, deliver, pin down,
nest, encompass, capture, formalize, rationalize, accommodate, tie together, rule out, recover,
characterize, identify.

**Favored idioms, each at most once per paper:** "bite" (CM15), "sharp predictions" (DEGO26),
"tight relationship" (DEGO26), "the devil is in the details" (CM15), "on the table" (CDM11), "tie
our hands" (EGOY24), "sink in" (DEGO26), "boost" (DEGO26), "off the shelf" (DM16), "ground truth"
(EGOY24), "linguistic shortcut" (DM16), "as-if account" (DEGO26), "hallmark" (DEGO26), "nearly
everywhere" (EGOY24), "garbling" (CLR20, technical).

**Scare quotes** on an informal or coined term at first use: "mistakes", "satisficing", "boost",
"simple points", 'constrained', 'limit case', 'too much'.

**Abbreviations after first use:** "decision maker (DM)", "cognitive uncertainty (CU)",
"henceforth CM15".

## 7. Punctuation and rhythm

- Sentence rhythm: a 5 to 12 word claim, then a 25 to 40 word explanation. Lists use parallel
  syntax ("that labor supply is an attenuated function of the wage, that investments are an
  attenuated function of expected returns, that savings vary with the interest rate in an attenuated
  way" EGOY24).
- Colons introduce the explanation or the list; semicolons separate list items that contain commas
  (EG23's abstract: "including the probability weighting function in choice under risk; base rate
  insensitivity, conservatism, and sample size effects in belief updating; and predictable
  overoptimism...").
- Em dashes: Enke and Dean use them for interjections; Anderlini and Felli and Chambers use
  parentheses. The thesis uses colons, semicolons and parentheses. Follow the document.
- Parentheses carry citations and quick asides: "(see also Caplin and Dean (2013))", "(e.g., Sims
  (2006), Woodford (2009))".
- Footnotes carry: scope restrictions, alternative assumptions, lists of references, caveats on
  terminology ("while we describe households ... as being 'irrational', this is really a linguistic
  shortcut" DM16).
- British spelling in CM15 (characterise, behaviour); American elsewhere. The thesis is American.

## 8. What the corpus never does

- Never opens with "In recent years" or "has attracted increasing attention".
- Never says "novel" twice, "to the best of our knowledge", "paves the way", "sheds light on",
  "of paramount importance", "bridges the gap".
- Never stacks "Moreover. Furthermore. Additionally." on consecutive sentences.
- Never uses "delve", "underscore" (verb), "tapestry", "landscape" (abstract), "pivotal",
  "crucial" as filler, "showcase", "testament", "intricate".
- Never ends a paragraph with a one-line aphorism that restates it.
- Never announces its own insight ("Here's the thing", "Let that sink in").
- Never writes "not merely X but Y" to inflate; a "not X but Y" appears only when X is a belief the
  reader holds (CD15: "this does not imply that decision makers are irrational").
- Never pads a contribution list with "extensive experiments" or "strong results"; each item names
  a specific result.
