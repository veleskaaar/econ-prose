# Preserve: what a generic humanizer would flip and econ-prose keeps

A ban list written for blog posts will damage an economics paper. Check this file before flagging.
When a corpus habit and a generic rule conflict, the corpus wins, in moderation.

| Keep | Why (and the limit) |
|---|---|
| "We" for the authors, including a single author | Standard in economics; never rewrite to "I" or to passive. |
| Passive voice when the actor is irrelevant | "Proofs are relegated to the appendix." "The state is drawn from a prior." Fine. Fix passives only when they hide who chooses or who pays. |
| Evidence-tied hedges: suggests, is consistent with, we conjecture, may, appears to | Correct where the claim is uncertain. Remove only stacked hedges on a proven result ("could potentially possibly"). |
| "Notice that", "Note that", "Clearly", "Of course", "Obviously", "It is immediate that" | Corpus habits (Anderlini and Felli, Caplin and Dean). Keep at most a few per section; "Clearly" must be clear. |
| "For simplicity", "For tractability", "Without loss of generality", "Unless noted otherwise", "Throughout the paper" | Standard assumption openers. Keep. |
| "Formally,", "In other words,", "That is,", "To sum up,", "Taken together," | Corpus connectives. Keep, not on consecutive sentences. |
| "First, ... Second, ... Third, ..." and "(i) ... (ii) ... (iii)" | The corpus enumerates this way constantly. A numbered list of real items is not a forced triad. |
| Question followed immediately by its answer | stop-slop bans it; the corpus uses it on purpose ("Which reference point provides more information? The status quo is..."). Keep, at most one or two per section. |
| Roadmap paragraph ("Section 2 presents... Section 3 ...") | Expected at the end of every introduction. Keep. |
| "Proofs are in the appendix." / "All proofs are relegated to the Appendix." | Convention. Keep. |
| Colons introducing an explanation, semicolons separating list items with internal commas | The thesis's own punctuation policy. Keep. |
| Scare quotes on a coined or informal term at first use ("shelter", "mistakes", "simple points") | Corpus habit. Keep on first use only. |
| A triad whose three items are three real things (three assumptions, three sources of uncertainty) | Not a tell. Flag only when the third item pads. |
| Short one-sentence paragraphs that state a result or a definition | Not a dramatic closer when the sentence carries a fact. |
| Model objects as subjects: "the permission raises both contacts", "chosen evidence pins down the beliefs", "the cost rises with" | Mechanisms act in a model. The false-agency rule applies only to vague agents ("the literature argues" when one paper is meant). |
| "decision maker (DM)", "henceforth CD15" abbreviations | Corpus habit. Keep. |
| Parenthetical citations and short parenthetical asides | The corpus uses parentheses freely. Keep; do not convert to dashes. |
| Formal register, no contractions, no "you" | The band is formal. Do not casualize. |
| The author's own verified habits | If earlier sections use "Importantly," or a semicolon every paragraph, those stay; edit toward the author, not toward a generic human. |
| Technical uses of banned words | "robust" (robust comparative statics, robust predictions), "key" in "key lemma", "landscape" in a fitness-landscape model, "leverage" as a financial term, "gate" in a gating mechanism. Keep technical uses. |
| An em dash or en dash in a document that already uses them consistently | Match the document's rate. The thesis uses none; keep it that way. |
| Numbers written as the author wrote them | Never round, reformat, or move a number. |

## Corpus phrases that look like tells and are not

These appear in the source papers and may be used sparingly:

- "It is important to notice at this point that" (AF00), "What matters for our purposes here is" (AF00)
- "Perhaps our most interesting findings are that" (DM16), "What is perhaps most striking is that" (EGOY24)
- "The devil is in the details" (CM15), "bite" (CM15), "sharp predictions" (DEGO26), "on the table" (CDM11)
- "Two remarks are in order." (DEGO26), "Two caveats are in order." (Enke review)
- "We are agnostic about" (DEGO26, EG23), "We deliberately exclude" (DEGO26)
- "This is an as-if account" (DEGO26)

Use each at most once per paper. Twice is a mannerism; three times is a parody.
